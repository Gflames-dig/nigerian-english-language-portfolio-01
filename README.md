2. ASR TRANSCRIPTION AND CORRECTION

Objective: Improve the accuracy of first-pass Automatic Speech Recognition transcripts while preserving the speaker's intended meaning and linguistic variety.

Demonstration Sample 1: Nigerian Pidgin

Synthetic ASR transcript:

"I no fit come today because I dey go market with my sister."

Reviewed verbatim transcript:

"I no fit come today because I dey go market with my sister."

Optional normalized English translation:

"I can't come today because I'm going to the market with my sister."

Linguistic observations:

- Preserved the original Nigerian Pidgin-influenced construction.
- Avoided unnecessary grammatical standardization.
- Maintained the original meaning.
- Distinguished verbatim transcription from translation.

Demonstration Sample 2: Standard English Grammar Correction

Synthetic first-pass ASR transcript:

"The meeting was suppose to start by eight but the speakers hasn't arrive."

Corrected transcript:

"The meeting was supposed to start by eight, but the speakers haven't arrived."

Corrections:

- "suppose" corrected to "supposed".
- "hasn't" corrected to "haven't".
- "arrive" corrected to "arrived".
- Added appropriate punctuation.

Quality principle: Corrections should improve transcript accuracy without unnecessarily changing the speaker's meaning or intended wording.

Note: These are synthetic portfolio demonstrations, not actual client recordings or completed commercial assignments.

3. NIGERIAN DIALECT AND ACCENT AWARENESS

Nigeria has diverse regional language varieties and pronunciation patterns.

My approach to dialect annotation recognizes the importance of distinguishing language variety from individual pronunciation characteristics.

Relevant varieties and influences include:

- Yoruba-influenced English.
- Igbo-influenced English.
- Hausa-influenced English.
- Edo English.
- Ijaw English.
- Efik English.
- Tiv English.
- Nigerian Pidgin-influenced English.

Annotation principles:

1. Identify observable linguistic features from the audio.
2. Distinguish accent, dialect, language, and code-switching.
3. Avoid assigning a speaker's ethnic identity based solely on pronunciation.
4. Follow the project's approved dialect taxonomy.
5. Record uncertainty where the evidence is insufficient.
6. Preserve meaningful regional language characteristics.

4. SPEAKER IDENTIFICATION AND AUDIO METADATA

Accurate audio annotation requires more than transcription. It also involves identifying speaker turns and recording relevant information about the audio.

Sample Metadata Annotation Template

Metadata Field| Annotation Approach
Recording ID| Unique recording identifier
Speaker ID| Consistent speaker identification
Timestamp| Beginning and ending time of speech
Language| Language supported by the recording
Dialect| Evidence-based dialect classification
Accent| Observable pronunciation characteristics
Speaking style| Conversational, formal, hesitant, etc.
Audio quality| Clear, distorted, muffled, etc.
Background noise| Traffic, music, environmental noise, etc.
Speaker overlap| Simultaneous speech when audible
Confidence| Confidence based on the evidence and rubric

My approach: I prioritize consistency, accurate timestamps, evidence-based speaker identification, and reliable metadata classification.
